LEGAL, BUT UNETHICAL

Consider also the case of John, a televangelist who preaches to thousands of people on his own nonprofit network. John and those involved in his organization know that the majority of the contributions they receive are from people who cannot afford it. They also know that a great many of the funds collected go first to the administrative overhead of the ministry, never benefitting those whose sad stories they use to solicit those contributions. The administrative overhead includes generous salaries, benefits, and allowances for those involved. Evidence of the generosity of those from whom they solicit is clear and the ministry receives endorsed social security checks each month from senior citizens whose income is marginal. While the operation may aim to remain within the letter of the law in its accounting practices, the ethical implications in conducting this type of charity are obvious. The televangelist must know when he pleads for funds for the poor that most of the funds will serve to support his lavish lifestyle. Yet John has done nothing overtly illegal. Unethical conduct is not always illegal. It may just skirt the law. Occasionally, illegal activities can be conducted for ethical reasons. An ethical analysis of a specific scenario can be complex, but in many instances, it is made easier by the existing laws and regulations.
