FEDERAL REGULATION

The companies may not have actually acquiesced, but in reality, we do have fifty-one regulators today. Despite McCarran-Ferguson, the federal government injects itself into the insurance regulation act quite regularly these days. The Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA) is one example of a federal law that impacts many of the products offered by life insurers. Increasingly, however, federal regulations are proliferating in other areas of financial services.
The reasons for increased federal intervention are many, but one is certainly the proliferation of variable insurance and annuity products over the past few years. Variable products themselves are not new, but the number of new products and sales have exploded in the past few years. The huge increase in sales of these products has opened the door wide for the Security and Exchange Commission (SEC) to step in. And they have indeed stepped in with both feet.
Another federal agency that receives the industry’s attention from time to time is the U.S. Treasury Department. Every once in a while someone at treasury suddenly “discovers” that the inside build-up of cash value in life insurance and annuities is not subject to current income taxation. The result of this discovery is usually a flurry of noisy threats of tax policy changes that require industry counter action. Fortunately, thus far the advantages and benefits of keeping life insurance and annuity cash value tax-deferred have carried the day.
