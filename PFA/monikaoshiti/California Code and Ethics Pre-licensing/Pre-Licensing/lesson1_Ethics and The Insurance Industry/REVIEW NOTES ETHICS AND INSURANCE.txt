REVIEW NOTES: ETHICS AND INSURANCE

ETHICS IN THE INSURANCE INDUSTRY
 Ethics is a set of moral principles or values that guides people’s behavior
 Within a profession, ethics refers to the code of conduct that governs that group’s actions such as how members interact with outsiders and with one another
 When insurance agents demonstrate a lack of ethics in business dealings, the entire industry may suffer from loss of reputation and stigma
 Empathy is a key component of how we treat others
 The concepts of empathy towards others and treating others as you would want to be treated are the foundation of ethical behavior.
 Ethics for the agent means treating prospects, clients, home office personnel — and even other agents — as they, themselves, would like to be treated. Such treatment includes honesty, fairness, and compassion
 Producers (and their insurers) must take the time to think about what constitutes an appropriate standard of behavior before they find themselves in situations that test it
 Producers have a fiduciary duty and responsibility to the consumer and to the insurer with whom it places insurance business
 A firm must provide a means for employees to seek assistance if they are being pressured into doing something unethical
ILLEGAL, BUT ETHICAL?
 Rules, standards, and laws protect us from liability and harm, but do not always reconcile with the practical impulses that we face every day
LEGAL, BUT UNETHICAL
 Unethical conduct is not always illegal. It may just skirt the law.
AGENT / COMPANY RELATIONSHIPS AND AGENT AUTHORITY
 State insurance law describes the ethical responsibilities of producers and the concept of agency
 The concept of “agency” is the legal relationship between two parties when one of the parties acts on behalf of another
 Expressed or Actual authority is the type of authority an agent possesses which is defined or contained in the agent contract or agreement
 Implied or “unwritten” authority is not specifically identified in the agency agreement. However, it is an extension of an agent’s regular duties
 Apparent authority is the type of authority the insurance buying public perceives the agent possesses based on his or her actions
MARKET CONDUCT
 Market conduct involves the measurement of an insurer and its producers with regard to marketing insurance products to consumers
 Insurers must be examined at least once every five years to determine their financial condition, their ability to meet its obligations, if they have complied with State law, the equity of their dealings with policyholders and any other information relating to their business practices and methods
 The purpose of state laws regarding disclosure letters and forms is to require insurers to deliver information to purchasers of insurance
 All insurers must maintain at its home or principal office a complete file containing one copy of each disclosure document authorized by the insurer
ETHICAL MARKETING PRACTICES
 All insurers and their producers would agree that there are ethical implications to the way in which insurance products are marketed
 Ethical marketing practices can be described as practices that emphasize competent, trustworthy, and responsible practices and acts that exhibit integrity and fairness to consumers
 All insurers should provide the same quality service to its consumers that it would demand for itself, with competence, fairness and honesty
 Insurers should also engage in competition fairly
 The Commissioner shall regulate all advertising in this State with regard to the sale or marketing of insurance, annuities, and endowments
 Advertising materials should always be written in a manner that can be easily comprehended by the average consumer. The contents of advertising materials must be true and not misleading
 Great customer service cements the relationship between the insurer and the consumer
 State insurance law describes regulations pertaining to consumer complaints. A complaint is an expression of grievance against an insurer
APPROPRIATE CLAIM PRACTICES
 Insurers must also, by law, maintain reserves to pay covered claims
 They must pay all covered claims in a timely fashion
 Insurers are not permitted to engage in any unfair claim practices
 If committed by an insurer, the Commissioner may suspend the insurer’s certificate of authority
 Any employees of the insurer who are responsible for unfair claim practices may be disciplined as well
REGULATION OF THE INSURANCE INDUSTRY
 There are two principal sources of regulation of insurance agent conduct:
o State insurance departments; and
o various agencies of the federal government
 The federal government injects itself into the insurance regulation act quite regularly
 State oversight of the insurance industry falls into two broad categories:
o statutes passed by the State legislators; and
o regulations that flow from the powers of the insurance department itself.
 In most States, the Commissioner (or Superintendent) of insurance has the power to issue rules and other forms of regulations that carry the full force of the law
THE RISE OF CONSUMER PROTECTION
 Consumer advocacy developed into a fine art and a major influence on our society
 Many states created a department of consumer advocacy, or a consumer ombudsman, to fight the good fight against consumer rip off and fraud
 By the 1990s, the new age of the consumer had fully dawned, bringing with it a new breed of customer. One that is better informed, more sophisticated, more demanding, and more litigious.
CLASS ACTIONS IN A LITIGIOUS SOCIETY
 Attorneys are uncovering the problems, they are not creating them
 Most companies, seeking to avoid the legal expenses and bad publicity of a court fight, settle as quickly as possible
CHOOSING AN INSURER
 In choosing a company to represent, it is certainly appropriate for the agent to consider how the company treats its own agents
 Agents deserve no less ethical treatment than customers or other company employees
 there is absolutely no justification for home office employees treating agents as less than full partners in fulfilling the company’s mission.
 Regardless of whether a particular agent has a high income or low income, he is entitled to promptly receive the agreed upon pay for the work performed
