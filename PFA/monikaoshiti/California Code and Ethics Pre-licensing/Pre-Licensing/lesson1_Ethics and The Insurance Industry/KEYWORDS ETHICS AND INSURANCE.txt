KEYWORDS: ETHICS AND INSURANCE

Agency: The legal relationship between two parties when one of the parties acts on behalf of another.

Apparent Authority: The type of authority the insurance buying public perceives the agent possesses based on his or her actions.

Certificate of Authority: The documenting authorizing an insurer to transact insurance in a state.

Complaint: An expression of grievance against an insurer.

Empathy: The ability to understand and share the feelings of another.
Ethical marketing practices: Practices that emphasize competent, trustworthy, and responsible practices and acts that exhibit integrity and fairness to consumers.

Ethics: A set of moral principles or values that guides people’s behavior.

Express Authority: The type of authority an agent possesses which is defined or contained in the agent contract or agreement.

Implied Authority: “Unwritten” authority is not specifically identified in the agency agreement. Any business activities that are usual and customary are implied.

Insurance Marketplace Standards Association: This association exists to promote fair and ethical treatment of policyholders on the part of insurance companies.

Insurer: A person or corporation that provides contracts of insurance. (Also called the principle)

Market Authority: The measurement of an insurer and its producers with regard to marketing insurance products to consumers.

Producer: A person who transacts insurance business on behalf of an insurer. (Also called an agent)
