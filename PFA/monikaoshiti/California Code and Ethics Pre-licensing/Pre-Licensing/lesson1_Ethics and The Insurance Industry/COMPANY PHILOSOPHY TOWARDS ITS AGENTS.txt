COMPANY PHILOSOPHY TOWARDS ITS AGENTS

In choosing a company to represent, it is certainly appropriate for the agent to consider how the company treats its own agents. Another variation of the golden rule will help us here. “Choose a company whose other employees treat its agents as they would want to be treated themselves.” Agents deserve no less ethical treatment than customers or other company employees. Of course, that means the agents have to treat home office employees likewise. If you are the agent who is constantly badgering underwriters or taking out your frustrations on whoever answers the telephone when you call the home office, you probably deserve the poor treatment you likely receive. On the other hand, there is absolutely no justification for home office employees treating agents as less than full partners in fulfilling the company’s mission.
Areas of concern would include the general attitude of home office employees towards agents. Agents should be perceived as customers of the various home office units that interact with them, and not adversaries or a necessary evil. Often, there is very little understanding of the agent’s job by employees “on the inside.” The agent’s job is sometimes perceived, by individuals unfamiliar with its daily routine, as an easy life. The agent doesn’t have to “punch a clock,” gets to set his own hours, and sometimes seems to be overpaid for the amount of actual work done. Lack of respect for the agent’s paycheck is another bad sign. An attitude of “it’s no big deal” if a policy’ isn’t issued as soon as it might be or if a commission is credited next pay period instead of this one, is unconscionable. Regardless of whether a particular agent has a high income or low income, he is entitled to promptly receive the agreed upon pay for the work performed. It’s incumbent upon the company to treat its agent fairly. It’s the right thing to do.
