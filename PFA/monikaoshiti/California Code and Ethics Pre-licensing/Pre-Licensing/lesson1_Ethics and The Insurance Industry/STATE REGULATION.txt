STATE REGULATION

State oversight of the insurance industry falls into two broad categories:

(1) statutes passed by the State legislators; and

(2) regulations that flow from the powers of the insurance department itself.

In most States, the Commissioner (or Superintendent) of insurance has the power to issue rules and other forms of regulations that carry the full force of the law. Most insurance Commissioners are appointed by the governor but, in some States, it is an elective position. Election, of course, introduces politics into the mixture in a big way. Election to the insurance Commissioner’s office is sometimes considered a springboard to higher State office, such as Governor. It’s easy to understand why. The Commissioner’s office can provide an ambitious politician with a platform for playing David to the insurance industry’s Goliath. Publicly “taking on” the insurance industry can play well in the press.
