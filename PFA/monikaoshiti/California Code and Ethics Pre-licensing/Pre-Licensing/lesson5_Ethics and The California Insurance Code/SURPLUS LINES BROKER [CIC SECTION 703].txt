SURPLUS LINES BROKER [CIC SECTION 703]

A surplus lines broker places insurance with insurers not licensed (i.e., nonadmitted) in California. According to Section 703 of the California Insurance Code, a surplus lines broker may perform the following services: act as agent for a nonadmitted insurer in the transaction of insurance business in California; advertise a nonadmitted insurer in California; and aid a nonadmitted insurer to transact insurance business in California. When not performed by a surplus lines broker, these acts are considered misdemeanors when performed in this State. In addition to any penalty provided for misdemeanors, a person violating this law must pay a $500 penalty, plus $100 for each month or fraction of a month during which he or she continues such violation.
In addition, Section 703 of the CIC states that any insurer transacting business or unlawfully acting as an insurer without a certificate of authority is punishable by imprisonment in a State or county prison of not more than one year, or by a monetary fine of not more than $100,000, or both.


UNLICENSED PERSONS [CIC SECTION 1631-1633]


Section 1631-1633 of the CIC states that any unlicensed person (individual or entity) who acts, offers to act or assumes to act in a capacity for which a license is required, is guilty of a misdemeanor. Except for a surplus lines broker, the following acts are misdemeanors in the State of California:


• acting as an agent for a nonadmitted insurer in the transaction of insurance;
• advertising a nonadmitted insurer in this State; or
• aiding a nonadmitted insurer to transact insurance in this State.


In addition to any penalty provided for committing a misdemeanor, a person violating this law must pay a monetary penalty of $500, plus $100 for each month or fraction of a month during which he or she continues such violations.


WRITTEN CONSENT IN REGARDS TO INTERSTATE COMMERCE (PROHIBITED PERSONS IN INSURANCE)


Federal Regulation 18 USC 1033 and 1034 stipulates that anyone who is engaged in the business of insurance whose activities affect interstate commerce and knowingly, with the intent to deceive, makes any false material statement or report, may be subject to a monetary penalty and imprisonment. Interstate commerce means any business conducted within the District of Columbia, any State within the U.S. or any of its territories or possessions. Any violation of this federal law will subject an individual to a monetary fine of up to $50,000, or imprisonment for up to ten (10) years, or both. In addition, if the material misrepresentation jeopardized the safety and soundness of an insurer and was a significant cause of the insurer being placed in conservation, rehabilitation or liquidation, the agent making the false statements may be subject to imprisonment of not more than fifteen (15) years. In other words, if the insurer’s solvency is threatened due to the material misrepresentations of a licensee, a prison sentence of up to 15 years may be assessed the guilty individual.


In addition, USC 18 1033 also describes the Violent Crime Control Act. It provides criminal and civil enforcement provisions for insurance fraud committed by persons in the insurance industry. The main aspect of this Act involves the fact that it is illegal for an individual convicted of a crime involving dishonesty, breach of trust or fiduciary responsibilities or a violation of the Act to work or continue to work in the insurance business without receiving written consent from an insurance regulatory official (i.e., Commissioner or Superintendent of Insurance). This Act contains no “grandfather provision” for persons already employed by, licensed in or transacting the business of insurance. Persons who fail to comply with this Act face federal fines and imprisonment.


PRODUCER LIABILITY FOR ERRORS AND OMISSIONS


Producers are exposed to liability for negligence in their duties as agents. Insurers purchase errors and omissions coverage in case they are sued by the insurance buying public due to the negligence or incompetence of their producers. Intentional acts by a producer would not be covered by this coverage.
A producer who has performed an unintentional error or makes an honest mistake has committed a tort known as an error and omission. This is why an insurer (or a producer) has a need for Errors and Omissions (E&O) insurance. The coverage available includes liability protection that will pay for defense costs and damages awarded to an injured party if the insurance professional is negligent while performing professional services. The professional services that E&O insurance cover includes the sale of insurance and employee benefit plans. Generally, E&O plans do not cover:
• fraud or any intentional criminal act committed by a producer;
• actions involving the misuse of client funds; or
• any actions that are covered by other insurance.
In addition, independent producers or agents generally purchase their own E&O coverage.


PROHIBITIONS OF FREE INSURANCE [CIC SECTION 777.1]


Section 777 of the CIC states that insurers may not offer free insurance or annuity contracts as an inducement to buy or rent any property or services. No producer or solicitor may arrange the sale of this type of insurance. However, this section of the CIC does not apply to:
• insurance written in connection with newspaper subscriptions;
• insurance issued to a credit union in connection with the purchase of shares in the credit union;
• insurance offered as a guarantee of the performance of goods;
• any title insurance or life or disability insurance written in connection with a loan, when the purpose of insurance is to pay the balance of the loan in the event the insured dies or becomes disabled;
• motor club insurance; or
• insurance provided incidentally to the sale of services if the cost of the insurance to the seller of the services does not exceed $1 per year for each purchaser.


If any insurer, agent, broker or solicitor wilfully violates the provisions of this article, the Insurance Commissioner may suspend or revoke his certificate or license or other authority to do business or engage in his occupation for a period not exceeding one year.
