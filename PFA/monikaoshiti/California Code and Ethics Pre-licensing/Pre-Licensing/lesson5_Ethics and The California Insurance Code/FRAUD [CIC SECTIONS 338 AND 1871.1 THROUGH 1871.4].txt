FRAUD [CIC SECTIONS 338 AND 1871.1 THROUGH 1871.4]

A fraud is an intentional and fraudulent omission on the part of an insured that an insured uses in an attempt to prove or support something false.
Insurers and their agents, while they are investigating suspected fraud claims, shall have access to all relevant public records that are required to be open for inspection. This does not grant insurers or their agents access to public records other than to those public records available to them under existing law.
Any insurer who, in connection with any insurance contract or provision of contract, prints, reproduces, or furnishes a form to any person upon which that person gives notice to the insurer or makes claim against it by reason of accident, injury, death, or other noticed or claimed loss, or on a rider attached thereto, shall cause to be printed or displayed in comparative prominence with other content the statement: “Any person who knowingly presents false or fraudulent claim for the payment of a loss is guilty of a crime and may be subject to fines and confinement in state prison.” This statement shall be preceded by the words: “For your protection California law requires the following to appear on this form” or other explanatory words of similar meaning.
This section is not applicable to a contract of reinsurance.
An insurer is entitled to rescind an insurance policy if an insured commits fraud.
Any person who knowingly files a false or fraudulent claim is guilty of a crime and may be subject to fines and confinement in state prison. All policies, except reinsurance contracts, carry a warning to this effect.
