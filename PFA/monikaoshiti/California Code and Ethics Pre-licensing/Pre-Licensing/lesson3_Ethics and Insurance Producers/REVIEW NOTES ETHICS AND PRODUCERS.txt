REVIEW NOTES: ETHICS AND PRODUCERS
ETHICAL CONDUCT
 Ethics involves morality issues. In other words, it involves what is right and wrong as applied to activities in any area of life including the insurance business
 Ethics is driven by a Code of Ethics
 An act committed by a person may be legal but it also may not be ethical
ETHICAL AND FIDUCIARY RESPONSIBILITIES OF INTERMEDIARIES / PRODUCERS
 A producer assumes a fiduciary responsibility with regard to an insurer and his or her clients
 The producer possesses the duty to act honestly and ethically with applicants for insurance coverage and to represent his or her insurer faithfully
 Producers must also strive to increase their knowledge with regard to financial services topics
 A producer who has made an unintentional error or honest mistake has committed a tort known as an error and omission. This is why an insurer purchases Errors and Omissions (E+O) insurance to cover the malpractice or negligence of producers
SUITABILITY OF PRODUCT TO THE CLIENT
 Suitability of product to the client means that the producer and insurer must make sure that the purchase of certain insurance policies are suitable for the purchaser
 Recommendations made by the producer are based upon information provided by the applicant or proposed insured
ETHICAL BEHAVIOR AND GOOD MORAL STANDARDS
 All producers must follow a specific code of conduct and meet moral standards when conducting all insurance transactions
 To be considered an ethical producer the holder of the license must maintain his or her integrity and perform insurance duties in a fiduciary capacity
 The public must be aware of the financial standing of insurers with which they do business by studying its solvency and liquidity
 The solvency of an insurer is an important issue since this involves the assets of an insurer being greater than its liabilities
 Liquidity involves the ability of the insurer to convert assets quickly to cash for any purpose including the payment of losses and other expenses
BINDING COVERAGE
 Insurance producers submit applications for coverage to the underwriting department of the insurer
 Some insurers utilize a binding receipt in some cases that binds coverage as of the date of the receipt
 Insurance protection will continue during the underwriting period and remain in effect until, and if, the application is declined and premium is refunded
BASIC ETHICAL PRINCIPLES
 The ethical process involves two steps:
o knowing what is right and what is wrong; and
o an individual must have the personal discipline, integrity and motivation to do what he or she knows is right.
 The Commissioner or Superintendent of Insurance, and his or her deputies, empowered with the responsibility of enforcing insurance laws that are on the books
 Honesty means to be straightforward and sincere
 An action that is against the law, or illegal, will usually be considered unethical. However, the opposite does not always hold. Being strictly legal does not necessarily guarantee that some action is also completely ethical
 Being trustworthy means being dependable and reliable to do the right thing
 Duty is the requirement to accept the personal accountability associated with professional responsibility and faithfully and persistently act ethically
 One of the greatest responsibilities that a person possesses is maintaining confidentiality of private or personal information
 Fairness involves following established and accepted standards, and avoiding personal judgments on others
 Conflicts of interest involve the abuse, actual or potential, of the trust placed in you as a professional
EVALUATING THE COURSE OF ACTION
 Any decision making process can be described as a series of steps. Like risk management, such steps can be summarized as:
o identifying and defining the issues;
o gathering information and collecting the facts;
o identifying the available options available;
o deciding what options to utilize;
o determining a course of action;
o implementing the decision; and
o monitoring and making changes over time.
EVERY AGENT’S DUTY
 Meeting the demands of industry regulators is the moral obligation of every insurance company
 Most insurance regulation revolves around disclosure and documentation
 Disclosure is the goal of most industry regulation, and documentation is the evidence they have that the goal is being pursued by the companies and their representatives
 Think of ethics as “doing the right thing” and compliance as “documenting that you have done the right thing.”
INTRODUCING YOUR COMPANY AND YOURSELF
 Self-disclosure is a tool for building trust
 Ethics is rooted in empathy and the need to treat others as we want to be treated
 The wise insurance sales professional will use various types of supporting materials or job aids to help the prospect understand the financial concepts being discussed
 Use only materials that have been approved by your company compliance department
 Agents are expected to be well informed on many tax and legal issues, but to be safe, your explanations and comments must fall short of providing legal and tax advice
CHOOSING SOLUTIONS
 There are two types of cases where insurance sales professionals may find themselves facing an ethical dilemma
o One is when the insurance need is uncomplicated and the agent is using a one-interview sales process
o the other is when the case is slightly more complex and requires more than one interview but does not require the help of other advisors
 In those two instances (the majority of most insurance agents’ work) there will be temptations to choose self-interest over client-interest
 A problem can arise if the agent has two similar term plans to propose and one has higher premium and pays a higher commission
 It would be very easy to sell the product that produces the most commission or leads to the most attractive sales convention. But ethical conduct rules call for the agent to use the product that best meets the needs of the client
 Always choose the product that works bests for the person you are serving and you will never go wrong
 A good method for keeping the proper ethical perspective on a case is to prepare two or three solutions (gold, silver and bronze) and let the prospect decide which solution is best
 Sales of products are required to meet strict suitability standards
CONSUMER UNDERSTANDING
 The agent must help the prospect understand the big picture of how insurance moves the prospect toward his or her financial goals
 It’s incumbent upon the agent to reveal to the prospect the risks involved
THE ETHICS OF REBATING AND REPLACEMENT
Almost every State has a law against insurance agents returning part of a premium to the insurance applicant as a rebate
Rebating insurance premiums is a case of a behavior being illegal but not necessarily unethical
The practice has been proven to deliver poor results for agents, companies, and consumers
Replacement very often does more harm than good
Since replacement of an existing policy is not always bad for the policyholder, it is not outlawed completely by the States. Rather, replacement regulations tends to focus on disclosure.
