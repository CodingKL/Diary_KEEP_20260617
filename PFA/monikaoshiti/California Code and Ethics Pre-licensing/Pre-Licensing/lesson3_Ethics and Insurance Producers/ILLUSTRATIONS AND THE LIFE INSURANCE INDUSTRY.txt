ILLUSTRATIONS AND THE LIFE INSURANCE INDUSTRY

There is another aspect to the illustration abuse story. The agent is not alone playing the illustration game. A large share of responsibility for improper illustrations used in the past must be borne by the companies themselves. Life insurance illustration software is an extremely complicated piece of work. It’s only the power of today’s computers that makes it seem so innocuous. In order to give the agent the power to create inaccurate illustrations, the software has to be designed with that capability in place. It’s virtually impossible for an agent to manipulate illustration software beyond what’s allowed by the software design. Many companies have recognized that fact and redesigned software with limitations built in to reduce or eliminate game-playing on the part of an unscrupulous agent.
Secondly, some companies have deliberately built unrealistic mortality projections or other gimmicks into their illustration software to improve the numbers. The agents could not possibly know of these tricks unless the company tells them. Only the actuaries and programmers developing the software know exactly how the computations are being made. If they include an assumption, for example, that the company’s mortality experience will somehow magically improve in the 19th year, there’s no way an agent or prospect (or advisors to the prospect) could possibly know. Someone who can find out, however, is the class action lawyer who subpoenas the company’s records on behalf of a lynch mob of policyholders, angry about their policy’s poor performance.
