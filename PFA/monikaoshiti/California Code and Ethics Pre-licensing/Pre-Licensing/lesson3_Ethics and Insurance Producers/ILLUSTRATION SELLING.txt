ILLUSTRATION SELLING

In Willy Loman’s day, selling insurance was a simple process. The agent has a rate book that contained all the rates for every plan the company offered. If the plan was participating, the rate book even contained projections of the value added to the policy via dividends. Incredibly, this was often contained in a rate book small enough to fit in a shirt or jacket pocket! The agent simply found the correct age and gender page, located the rate per thousand, and multiplied it by the number of thousands being proposed. The worst thing that would happen is the agent would occasionally forget to carry the right one and the quoted rate would turn out to be incorrect.
