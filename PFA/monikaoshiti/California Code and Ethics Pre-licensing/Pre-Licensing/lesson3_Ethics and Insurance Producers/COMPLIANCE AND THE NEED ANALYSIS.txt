COMPLIANCE AND THE NEED ANALYSIS

There is a role for compliance in the fact and feeling process as well. Financial concepts are often difficult for people to understand. The wise insurance sales professional will use various types of supporting materials or job aids to help the prospect understand the financial concepts being discussed. For example, you might use glossy printed pages or graphic computer images to help make your point from time to time. Or maybe you carry newspaper clippings of items you want to use to bolster your point of view. Every word on this supporting material may very well be true, but do you know that for sure? Many of the third-party materials that you might be tempted to use in the interview could cause compliance problems. Illustrations showing net cost comparisons of term insurance versus permanent insurance, for example, are generally frowned upon by regulators since they usually ignore the time value of money.
It’s easy to fall into the trap of using weak (or nonexistent) “facts” if they seem to work. You owe it to your prospects to use truthful information to persuade or enlighten. Use only materials that have been approved by your company compliance department. If you use materials you prepare yourself, make certain the “facts” you represent in them are real. Ask yourself, “Would I be able to defend this presentation in court, if I had to?”
Another compliance related area to avoid is playing tax advisor or attorney during the fact and feeling-finding interview. Agents are expected to be well informed on many tax and legal issues, but to be safe, your explanations and comments must fall short of providing legal and tax advice. Whenever a legal or tax question arises concerning a prospect’s specific situation, the wise agent recommends consulting an attorney or tax advisor. Remember, you won’t earn any commissions sitting in a courtroom defending yourself against a charge of practicing law without a license.
